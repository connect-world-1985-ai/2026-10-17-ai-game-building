Worked example: a 3D car racer

A 3D racer is a step up from a platformer: a third dimension, a camera that can make players sick, vehicle physics, and 3D models instead of sprites. This page shows how to keep it small enough to finish, and includes a tiny racer built entirely from code.

Play the mini racer

About 170 lines of JavaScript on top of Three.js. The track is a closed spline turned into a road mesh; the car is four boxes and four cylinders; there are no model or texture files. Physics is "arcade": speed, steering and grip are simple formulas, not a simulation. Verified (our demo)

The 3D demo needs JavaScript and an internet connection for Three.js.

Three.js r149 is loaded from the jsDelivr CDN with a pinned version and an integrity hash. If it cannot load, the box above explains why; the rest of the page works offline.

Scope: the smallest version worth making

Must have

One car with good arcade handling; one closed track; a chase camera; checkpoints and lap timing; a "best lap" display; off-road slowdown; restart.

Next

Two or three AI opponents following a racing line; a start countdown; position display; engine sound that follows speed; a minimap.

Later

More tracks, car choice, drifting and boost, ghost replay of your best lap, gamepad support, collisions between cars.

Architecture

Arcade or simulation physics?

ApproachHow it worksGood forCost
Arcade formulas (the demo)Position along a heading; speed changes with throttle and drag; steering turns the heading; "grip" blends velocity toward the headingKart and casual racers; the first version of anythingNo physics library; easy to tune and test; fully deterministic
Raycast vehicleOne rigid body for the chassis; each wheel is a ray to the ground with spring, damper and frictionBumps, jumps, ramps, car-to-car collisionsMany parameters to tune; flipping and jitter; needs a physics engine
Full simulationTyre models (for example Pacejka curves), drivetrain, weight transferSim racersExpert work; out of scope for a first game

Raycast vehicles exist in the common engines: RaycastVehicle in cannon-es (no commits since 2024-01), a ray-cast vehicle controller in Rapier (the JS bindings repo was archived in 2026 and merged into the main Rapier repo), and VehicleBody3D in Godot. Unity uses WheelCollider. Verified (2026-10-17)

Recommendation: start with arcade formulas. Add a raycast vehicle only when the game needs jumps or bumps; arcade handling is what most players of casual racers expect anyway. Inference

Build order with an agent

  1. Scene. "Single page, Three.js pinned to one version, a ground plane, lights, fog, a resize handler, a fixed-step loop and a debug overlay."
  2. Car on flat ground. Arcade controller with every number in a config object. Drive it, tune it yourself.
  3. Chase camera. Smoothed follow behind and above, look slightly ahead. Test on a big screen and a phone; fast camera rotation causes motion sickness.
  4. Track. Closed spline → road ribbon, edge markers, off-road slowdown measured by distance to the spline.
  5. Race logic. Checkpoints that must be passed in order (otherwise players cut corners), lap counter, lap timer, best lap.
  6. AI opponents. Steer toward a point a few metres ahead on the racing line; vary speed per opponent. Unit-test that an AI car finishes a lap.
  7. Assets and audio. Swap in Kenney cars and track pieces; engine sound whose pitch follows speed.
  8. Polish and ship. Countdown, results screen, touch controls, performance check on a low-end laptop, itch.io build.

Inference, following the practice on pages 7 and 10.

The hard parts

ProblemTypical symptomFix
Handling feelCar feels like ice, or turns like a tankSpeed-dependent steering, grip that blends velocity toward heading, play and tune by hand
CameraJitter, clipping into walls, nauseaSmooth with exponential damping in the fixed step; limit rotation speed; raise the camera at speed
Corner cuttingLaps counted without driving the trackOrdered checkpoints; reset to the last checkpoint if off-track too long
Physics instability (raycast vehicle)Car flips, bounces or vibrates at restLower the centre of mass, tune suspension, smaller physics step; or stay arcade
Agent can't see the game"Fixed" bugs that are not fixedText state hook, screenshots, scripted laps (page 10); headless WebGL may render black, so test headed
PerformanceStutter on laptops and phonesInstancing for repeated props, few lights, shadows off at first, cap pixel ratio at 2

Practice. The headless-WebGL caveat comes from Playwright testing reports (page 10).

What one well-documented agent run produced

At the Codex app launch on 2026-02-02, OpenAI said Codex built a Three.js voxel kart racer with "different racers, eight maps, and items" from "one initial user prompt", using an image-generation skill and a web-game skill, and "more than 7 million tokens". It published the prompt, the skills and earlier iterations. The follow-up turns were automatic, scripted continuations, so this is "one human prompt", not "one model response". Verified (that OpenAI says this) / Practice (reproducibility). See page 11.
